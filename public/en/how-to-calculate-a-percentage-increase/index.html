How to calculate a percentage increase

A percentage increase tells you how much a value has grown relative to where it started. The same method works for a decrease. You can do the arithmetic by hand or use the percentage calculator on Free Online Calculator.

Steps

  1. Write down the old and new values. Call the starting figure the original amount and the later figure the new amount. Use the same units for both.
  2. Subtract, then divide by the original. Percentage change is ((new - original) / original) x 100. A positive result is an increase; a negative result is a decrease.
  3. Check the result in the calculator. Open the percentage calculator on Free Online Calculator and enter the same two numbers to confirm the percentage.
  4. Say what the percentage refers to. When you report the figure, mention the original amount and the period, for example a price change from one month to the next.

A worked example

If a price moves from 200 to 250, the change is 50. 50 / 200 = 0.25, which is a 25 percent increase. If it moves from 250 back to 200, the decrease is 50 / 250 = 0.20, or 20 percent. The two percentages are not the same because the starting points differ.

Common mistakes

Do not divide by the new amount when you want a change from the original. Do not treat percentage points (a change from 10 percent to 12 percent is 2 percentage points) as the same thing as a 2 percent change.
